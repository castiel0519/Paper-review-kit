1. Introduction
All-inorganic
perovskite
quantum
dots
(PQDs),
typically
CsPbX3(X ¼ Cl/Br/I)
PQDs,
have
shown
great
application
potentials in solar cells,[1–3] lasers,[4,5] displays,[6,7] computing,[8,9]
catalysis[10–13] and detectors,[14–18] etc. To date, wet-chemical
strategies of hot injection,[19,20] room-temperature reaction,[21]
solvothermal,[22]
microwave,[23]
ion
exchange,[24] ultrasonic,[25] and microﬂui-
dic[26,27] methods have been developed to
prepare PQDs. PQDs with controllable
photoluminescence (PL) wavelength can be
synthesized, due to the adjustable bandgap
from
quantum
conﬁnement
effect,
tailorable component, and high defect
tolerance.[28–35] In the effort to achieve tun-
able optical and electrical properties, the
synthesis methods and reaction parameters
such as precursors, reagent ratios, temper-
ature, reaction time, and solution system
need to be properly selected and precisely
controlled. The critical issue that we must
confront is how to ﬁnd the optimal param-
eters in the large parameter space contain-
ing many continuous and discrete variables.
To overcome this challenge, basically there are two routes. From
the hardware end is to enhance the experimental efﬁciency (e.g.,
high throughput) and robustness of the method. From the
software end is to develop an artiﬁcial intelligence (AI) system
to predict the product properties by the reaction parameters or
vice versa. Obviously, a combination of these two strategies would
boost scientiﬁc research and application pace of the PQDs.
The thermal injection route to synthesize PQDs is the most
commonly used method. However, the batch differences and
low throughput hinder the large-scale production and optimi-
zation of the synthesis.[26] In recent years, microﬂuidic technol-
ogy has been rapidly promoted in new quantum dots
discovering and mass production.[36–40] The change of reaction
parameters can be controlled rapidly with good reproducibility.
Due to the limitation of the reaction space, the rapid heat trans-
fer can be realized, which is beneﬁcial toward high-quality
PQDs.[41] Owing to the automatic synthesis procedure, the
uncontrollable human operation factors are largely avoided.
Also, the higher throughput and efﬁciency make it possible
to reveal continuous inline big data collection for optimiza-
tion.[42] During the last 60 years, the AI have been applied to
chemistry in many subﬁelds.[43] It liberates researchers from
laboring analysis of large amount of data with complicated
relationships, which is one of the major characters in synthetic
chemistry due to the complexity of kinetics and dynamics in
chemical systems.[44] As one of the powerful branches of
AI, machine learning (ML) has shown great potential for
chemical science. Speciﬁcally, ML has been applied to investi-
gate the stability,[45–48] bandgap,[49–51] Curie temperature,[52]
power conversion efﬁciency,[53] microstructures,[54] shape,

The quality and property control of nanomaterials are center themes to guarantee
and promote their applications. Different synthesis methods and reaction
parameters are control factors for their properties. However, the vast combination
number of the factors with multilevels leads to the obstacle that trying all-through
the data space is nearly impossible. Herein, the combination of microﬂuidic
synthesis method with machine learning (ML) models to address this challenge in
case of perovskite quantum dots (PQDs) with tunable photoluminescence (PL) is
reported. The ML-assisted synthesis not only helps to elucidate the nucleation
growth-ripening mechanisms, but also successfully guides to synthesize PQDs
with precise wavelength and full width of half maximum (FWHM) of the PL by
optimizable conditions to match the time-saving, energy-saving, and minimal
environmental pressure goals.

Herein, we developed an ML system guiding the synthesis

(FWHM) of the PL spectrum. In addition to the Pb:Cs molar
ratio and reaction halogen ratio, reaction temperature and ﬂow

which is advantageous for the tuning of PQD size and size dis-
tribution with more degrees of freedom. A total of 470 micro-

ing ML models. The PL data were off-line measured (in 4 h)
by a spectrometer. Before the PL measurement, the PQDs
were separated from the reaction solution by centrifugation
and redispersed in cyclohexane to form a colloidal solution.

for the PQDs must be puriﬁed from the reagents and byprod-

type and concentration, ligands, and else impurities would

synthesis solution may deviate a lot from puriﬁed PQDs.
With the trained ML system, the PL wavelength (bandgaps
of 1.8–3.1 eV) and FWHM were predicted by traversing the
synthesis parameter space. The ML results elucidated synthe-
sis mechanisms and showed satisfactory prediction accuracy,
facilitating the synthesis optimization with high efﬁciency.
Speciﬁcally, reaction parameter optimization can be achieved
with different restrictions in addition to wavelength and
FWHM, for example, to synthesize PQDs with certain PL in
the shortest time, at lowest temperature, or using minimal lead
to alleviate environmental pressure. The schematic illustration
of the AI-assisted microﬂuidic PQD synthesis system is shown

2. Results and Discussion
2.1. Microﬂuidic Synthesis of PQDs
The size, morphology, and PL spectrum of microﬂuidic
synthesized CsPbX3(X ¼ Cl/Br/I) PQDs can be tuned by the
reaction temperature, ratio of precursors, microchannel diame-
ter, and ﬂow rate. Typical transmission electron microscopy
(TEM) images of these PQDs are shown in Figure 2. Some
PL FWHM–wavelength–temperature curves are plotted in
Figure 3. The PL wavelength is highly correlated to the bandgap,
which is controlled not only by the size/morphology according to
quantum conﬁnement effect, but also by the components such as
the ratio of halides. The PL FWHM is sensitive to the component
and size/morphology distribution. The PL properties are formed
and changed during the PQD nucleation, growth, and ripening
processes. These processes are principally kinetic controlled in
most cases. They are often very complicated, partially because
the reaction mechanism can hardly be described thoroughly,
for example, many incompletely known intermediate products
such as monomers/oligomers/byproducts of different species
and conﬁgurations that could be involved, matter transfer issues
of diffusion-thermal/forced convection, catalytic effects, surface
reconstruction and self-assembly process, etc. The complicated
relationships between the wavelength, FWHM, and reaction
parameters (Figure 3g) suggest a highly nonlinear kinetic reac-
tion system far from thermodynamic equilibrium. Therefore,
using traditional simple models with clear physical–chemical
pictures is largely restricted. The machine learning could be a
better choice.
One-phase ﬂow microﬂuidic synthesis was adopted in this
study. Commonly, a two-phase ﬂow microﬂuidic synthesis uses
an additional immiscible ﬂuid/gas, which would eradicate foul-
ing of the channel wall and reduce the affect of the residence
time distributions caused by the velocity proﬁle in the cross
section of the channel. However, the phase mode seems not a
critical factor here. First, the reaction channel here is the poly-
tetra ﬂuoroethylene capillaries, where the inner wall remains
uncontaminated for this reaction system. Second, narrow size
distribution of the PQDs could also be achieved despite the veloc-
ity proﬁle. In addition, one-phase ﬂow provides synthetic ﬂexibil-
ity and can withstand high ﬂow rates; More importantly, it allows
the introducing of additional reagents into multistep reactions
and facilitates the formation of more complex structures.[59–63]

the temperature, the higher the PQD growth rate could be, result-

FWHM. Comparatively, the ﬂow rate has bigger inﬂuence on
FWHM than on PL wavelength.
Although the heat map of Pearson correlation coefﬁcient
and the according histogram give guidelines in searching for
synthesis parameters, it is deﬁcient for precise prediction. It
can point out a synthesis direction roughly for a target
FWHM–wavelength, but cannot give any speciﬁc preparation
parameters. For example, to synthesis PQDs with a given PL
wavelength and narrow FWHM, it suggests to select the proper
halogen ratio ﬁrst and conduct the reaction under relatively high
temperature and low ﬂow rate. However, in relatively low tem-
perature or high ﬂow rate, the same result could also be achieved
practically, due to the complex synthesis mechanisms. With the
aid of ML algorithms, these deﬁciencies can be overcome based
on the prediction maps, which are discussed in the following
paragraphs.

3. Conclusion
In summary, ML algorithms were developed to assist the micro-
ﬂuidic synthesis of PQDs. The ML models show excellent ability
in visualizing of how the synthesis parameter controlled nucle-
ation growth ripening mechanisms of PQDs in the conditional
prediction maps. They exhibit powerful prediction ability in
searching for proper/optimized reaction conditions that synthe-
size PQDs with required FWHM–wavelengths. They are ready to
be extended to the prediction of more properties such as size dis-
tribution and PL quantum yields etc. The ML model could also be
adapted to nanomaterials beyond PQDs, facilitating 1) the design
and discovery of new nanomaterials; 2) understanding the syn-
thesis mechanisms; 3) online quality control; 4) property optimi-
zations; and 5) synthesis optimization for time saving, reagent
saving, energy saving, and environmental-friendly purposes,
thus accelerating the process of research and promoting the
industrialization of functional nanomaterials.
