INTRODUCTION The success of the miniaturization of microelectronics and computers inspired the initial developments in microﬂuidics.1 Analogously, microﬂuidic chips now offer the capabilities of entire laboratories in small footprints.2 Microﬂuidics operate by manipulating liquids within networks of microchannels with dimensions on the order of micrometers. Fluids can take the form of monophasic and multiphasic ﬂows or discrete microdroplets in the femto- to microliter scale (1015 to 106 L). Owing to their small dimensions, microﬂuidic systems display a range of unique physical char- acteristics.3 As viscous forces dominate over inertial forces, ﬂows at low Reynolds number exhibit laminar proﬁles. Thus, mass, momentum, and heat transport in the direction normal to the ﬂow are restricted to the molecular mechanisms of diffusivity, viscosity, and thermal conductivity, respectively. The superior heat and mass trans- port enabled by the high surface-area-to-volume ratio allows for high control over reaction conditions and shorter reaction times.4 Moreover, simple microfabrication techniques using polydimethylsiloxane5 permit quick fabrication of chips with inherent chemical inertness, thermostability, and low toxicity. ## p27 [conclusion]
CONCLUSION The synergistic combination of microﬂuidic chips and machine-learning algorithms has created an emerging yet rapidly growing ﬁeld that promises to expand avenues for fundamental research in the chemical and biological sciences. Both of these ﬁelds are expected to beneﬁt from the adoption of intelligent microﬂuidic platforms. We dared to suggest potential applications and directions for research in the hope of inspiring researchers to explore the exciting possibilities enabled by the conver- gence of microﬂuidics and machine intelligence.
