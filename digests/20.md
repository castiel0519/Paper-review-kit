## p2 [abstract]
ABSTRACT Rapid, portable, and automated determination of minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) is critical for antimicrobial susceptibility testing in decentralized settings. We present TCG-CMDA (Tree-Shaped Concentration Gradient generator enabled by Capillary Microfluidic Design Automation), a machine learning–guided platform for the automated design of capillary microfluidic chips that passively generate programmable concentration gradients. A key innovation of TCG-CMDA is its ability to achieve fully synchronized, capillary-driven flow of two preloaded agents, enabling precise and reproducible gradient formation without external pumps or valves. By integrating computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations, a neural network surrogate model, and quasi-Newton optimization, the platform customizes tree-shaped geometries to match target mixing profiles. We validate TCG-CMDA through dye flow visualization and MIC testing of Escherichia coli against gentamicin, showing high concordance with conventional antibiotic susceptibility testing. The resulting chip supports a streamlined workflow consisting of passive loading, autonomous gradient generation, and incubation (∼2 h), followed by direct endpoint assessment, making it well suited for decentralized point-of-care applications. ## p5 [discussion]
Discussion This study presents the development and validation of CMDA platform to support the rational design and customization of TCG chips for programmable, on-chip MIC testing, with an emphasis on POC diagnostics (Scheme 1A). The TCG-CMDA platform leverages simulation-informed design rules to generate microfluidic layouts capable of forming predefined antibiotic gradients suited for a variety of biological and clinical targets. To achieve this, the platform systematically tunes geometrical parameters of the chip, the pitch and width of mixing com- ponents (MCs), to optimize convective partitioning, diffusive mixing, and uniform fluid distribution across all output channels (Scheme 1B). The TCG chip incorporates two inlets for preloading distinct agents: Agent 1, comprising microbial suspensions (e.g., E.coli or other biological samples), and Agent 2, representing therapeutic compounds such as antibiotics. In this study, both agents were aqueous LB-based suspensions with low viscosity and similar physicochemical properties, ensuring compatibility with spon- taneous capillary flow within the laminated PMMA/PSA/TPE architecture. Systematic evaluation across a broader range of viscosities and surface tensions was beyond the scope of this work and is reserved for future investigation. ## p17 [conclusion]
Conclusion This study introduces the Tree-Shaped Concentration Gradient Generator enabled by Capillary Microfluidic Design Automation (TCG-CMDA) which is a transformative platform that integrates capillary-driven fluidics, CFD, and neural network-guided opti- mization to autonomously generate programmable concentration gradients. Demonstrated in the context of MIC testing for E. coli and gentamicin, the TCG chip achieved high precision (<5% devi- ation) and reproducibility across replicates. Its passive, pump-free operation supports fully automated workflows without external instrumentation, requiring only a camera for readout, and is com- patible with low-cost, scalable manufacturing techniques such as laser cutting. The CMDA pipeline accelerates design iteration through a quasi-Newton optimization loop embedded within a machine learning surrogate model, establishing a reproducible, data-driven path for intelligent lab-on-chip design. Beyond its technical innovations, the TCG-CMDA chip holds immediate clinical value as a truly POC diagnostic tool. Its ability to rapidly assess susceptibility to a single first-line antibiotic can be life-saving in critical care situations such as sepsis, where each hour of delay in effective antibiotic administration increases mortality risk by 7.6%.
